# Definitions

Every term of the model, defined at implementer resolution.
Each definition names its Lean counterpart in `proof/`; the
Lean development is the authority wherever prose and code
could drift. Four states are kept distinct throughout:
settled intent, proposed semantics, implemented behavior,
verified behavior. Everything below is implemented behavior
in the Lean development unless a line says otherwise.

## Datum

A datum is the persistent object under analysis: a list of
parts, in order. Lean: a `List Nat`, the first argument of
`cost`, `Dominates`, `Minimal`, and the choice procedure
`choose` in `proof/Core.lean`. Two data are composed by
list append; cost additivity (`costadd`) is stated over
exactly that append.

## Part

A part is one component of a datum: a single natural
number, one position of the datum list. Axes price parts
individually (an axis is a function of a part and a choice
code), and versions are lists of parts. Lean: an element of
the datum `List Nat`; in a hyperedge, a premise or the
conclusion, each a `Nat`.

## Representation

A representation of a datum is a list of choice codes of
the same length as the datum, one code per part, in the
same order. The full candidate space of representations of
a fixed length over a choice alphabet is generated by
`reps` in `proof/Core.lean`: `reps ch 0 = [[]]`, and
`reps ch (n+1)` extends every representation of length `n`
by every code of the alphabet `ch`. Its size is the
computed power `ch.length ^ n` (theorem `finite`).

## Choice code

A choice code is one element of the contract's finite
choice alphabet: a natural number naming how the
corresponding part is held. Code zero is materialized —
the part itself is stored. Code `e+1` is functional — the
part is held as a derivation through recorded hyperedge
`e`. This reading of the codes is settled intent at the
campaign level; the Lean model prices codes through the
axes and does not itself inspect the reading.

## Hyperedge

A hyperedge is a recorded interaction: a pair of a premise
list and a conclusion, `(premises, conclusion)`, each a
list of parts and a part. Lean: an element of
`List (List Nat × Nat)`, the edge list argument of `Deriv`,
`runCert`, and `checkCert`. An edge is recorded when it
stands in the persistent edge list; whether a recorded
edge is faithful to a real derivation is a campaign-level
premise, named in `spec.md`, outside the Lean development.

## Persistent state

Persistent state is the pair of the stored facts and the
recorded hyperedges: a `List Nat` of facts present in the
store, and a `List (List Nat × Nat)` of recorded edges.
Every predicate of the model is relative to a persistent
state; growth of the state (more facts stored, more edges
recorded) is the only change the incremental theorems
consider.

## Derivability

A fact is derivable from a persistent state when it is
stored, or when some recorded edge concludes it and every
premise of that edge is derivable. Lean: the inductive
predicate `Deriv edges base c` in `proof/Core.lean`, with
constructors `Deriv.base` and `Deriv.step`. Derivability
is monotone in the store (`monof`) and in the recorded
edges (`monoe`), and adjoining a derivable fact to the
store changes the derivable set not at all (`elim`).

## Certificate

A certificate is a list of hyperedges, in order: the edges
a claimed derivation uses, naming only its premise cone.
Lean: a `List (List Nat × Nat)`, the last argument of
`checkCert`. Running a certificate (`runCert`) folds its
edges in order, at each step checking that every premise
is already in the accumulated store and adjoining the
conclusion; it returns success and the final store.

## Premise cone

The premise cone of a fact, relative to a certificate, is
the set of facts the certificate's run actually touches:
the initial store facts its first edges consume and the
intermediate conclusions its later edges consume. A
certificate names only its premise cone — nothing else in
the persistent state is inspected by the checker — which
is why certificates survive growth of the state
(`certmono`).

## Qualification contract

A qualification contract fixes the finite choice alphabet
and the list of cost axes against which representations
are qualified. Lean: the structure `Contract` in
`proof/Core.lean`, with fields `ch : List Nat` and
`axes : List (Nat → Nat → Nat)`. Finiteness of the
candidate space is derived from the contract as a computed
power (`finite`), never assumed as a hypothesis about a
larger space.

## Axis

An axis is a named cost dimension: a function assigning a
natural cost to each (part, choice code) pair. Lean: a
`Nat → Nat → Nat`, an element of the contract's `axes`
list. Axes are parameters of the contract, never fixed
constants of the model; control one in
`proof/Controls.lean` proves minimality is relative to
the axis list.

## Cost

The cost of a representation of a datum on one axis is the
sum over the zipped (part, code) pairs of the axis values.
Lean: `cost` in `proof/Core.lean`. Cost is additive over
append of data and representations, segment by segment,
when the segment lengths agree (`costadd`). The total
cost of a list of versions under one axis is `vcost`,
splitting at the head by `vcostcons`.

## Domination

One representation dominates another, for a datum under an
axis list, when it is at most on every named axis and
strictly under on at least one. Lean: the proposition
`Dominates` and the boolean check `domB`, tied by the
bridge theorem `dombridge`. Domination is irreflexive
(`domirr`) and transitive (`nodom`).

## Minimum

A representation is minimum (Pareto-optimal) for a datum
in a candidate list when it is a candidate and no
candidate dominates it. Lean: the proposition `Minimal`
and the boolean checker `minimalB`, whose soundness is
`mincheck`. A minimum of a composed whole has minimum
parts (`wholemin`); the converse fails, by the cross-part
tradeoff control (`controlfour`).

## Choice procedure

The choice procedure is the producer of minima: a fold
over the candidate list keeping an undominated candidate,
switching exactly when the challenger dominates the
current best. Lean: `keep` and `choose` in
`proof/Core.lean`. Its output is a minimum by construction
(`choosemin`, through the fold invariant `choosecore`),
so every nonempty candidate list carries a minimum
(`existsmin`).

## Redundant computation

A computation is redundant when its conclusion is already
derivable from the persistent state through recorded
hyperedges, with its premises inside the persistent
closure: formally, when the edge it would perform has its
conclusion in the derivable set. Step closure
(`stepclose`) is the criterion in theorem form: a recorded
edge whose premises are all derivable has a derivable
conclusion, so performing it adds nothing.

## Elimination

Elimination is the removal of a redundant computation,
justified by `elim` in `proof/Core.lean`: adjoining an
already derivable fact to the store changes the derivable
set not at all, in both directions — so the computation
that would have produced the fact can be dropped, and any
later use of the fact still resolves. Elimination is
sound exactly under the redundancy criterion; control two
(`controltwo`) proves an edge whose premise is outside
the closure eliminates nothing.

## Semi-persistence

Semi-persistence is the discipline of keeping older
versions readable as the store evolves: a version is a
datum, and it stays available exactly while each of its
parts stays derivable. The shared-premise case is the
load-bearing one: two versions deriving through a common
premise stay readable while that premise is kept, as a
stored fact or as a recorded interaction; dropping it
loses the versions that still need it (control three,
`controlthree`; positive direction, `kept`).

## Readable version

A version is readable from a persistent state when every
part of it is derivable from the stored facts through the
recorded edges. Lean: `Readable edges stored v` in
`proof/Core.lean`. Readability is monotone in the store
(`readmono`): growing the store never loses a readable
version.

## Updatable subset

The updatable subset of a persistent state is the set of
additions — newly stored facts and newly recorded edges —
under which everything already certified stays certified.
By `certmono` and `runmono`, checked certificates stay
checked under any such addition, and by `readmono`
readable versions stay readable; so the updatable subset
is exactly the growth cone of the state: additions only,
never removals or rewrites. A removal leaves the updatable
subset and must be re-qualified from scratch.

## Certificate checker versus producer

The checker and the producer are separate artifacts with
separate obligations. The checker is a boolean function —
`checkCert` for derivations, `minimalB` for minimality —
that any party can run on a claimed verdict and a
candidate artifact; its soundness theorems (`certsound`,
`mincheck`) tie an accepting check to the predicate. The
producer is whatever constructs the artifact — a
derivation search emitting a certificate, the fold
`choose` emitting a representation — and is trusted for
nothing: a verdict stands only when the checker accepts
and the soundness theorem applies. The checker is
monotone in the state (`certmono`); no property of any
producer is assumed anywhere in the development.
